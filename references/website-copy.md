# Website Copy — Homepage & Landing Page Conversion Rules

This skill writes full websites, not just blogs. This reference covers the two conversion-critical pages: the homepage and the landing page.

## The Core Principle

A homepage and a landing page have ONE job: move a visitor toward a single action. Every word either advances that action or gets cut. This is not a blog — it's a conversion asset.

**The difference:**
- **Homepage** = the front door. It has to serve multiple audiences (researchers, ready buyers, referrals) and route each toward the right next step.
- **Landing page** = a single offer, a single audience, a single CTA. No navigation, no distractions, one path.

---

## Homepage Writing Rules (High Conversion)

### The Above-the-Fold Structure (what a visitor sees before scrolling)

1. **Hero headline** — one sentence that says what you do AND who it's for, in the reader's language. Not "We provide solutions" — "We build revenue systems for service businesses."
2. **Subheadline** — one sentence that names the outcome. "Turn your website into a lead engine that ranks on Google and gets recommended by AI."
3. **Primary CTA** — one clear button. "Get a Free Audit" or "Book a Call." Not "Learn More."
4. **Social proof strip** — logos, numbers, or a one-line credibility marker. "Trusted by 100+ service businesses" or client logos.

### The Hero Headline Rules

- **Specific beats clever.** "We build websites that get you calls" beats "Digital excellence for the modern age."
- **Name the reader.** "For HVAC, roofing, and plumbing companies" beats "for businesses."
- **One idea.** Don't cram two messages into one headline.
- **No jargon.** If a stranger can't understand it in 3 seconds, rewrite it.

### The Homepage Sections (in order)

1. **Hero** — headline, subhead, CTA, social proof
2. **Problem** — name the pain the reader feels ("You're paying for ads and not getting calls")
3. **Solution** — what you do, in plain language, framed as the fix
4. **Proof** — case studies, numbers, testimonials, client logos
5. **Process** — how it works, in 3-4 simple steps
6. **Objection handling** — answer the top 2-3 fears ("I've been burned before," "How much does it cost?")
7. **Final CTA** — the one action, repeated

### Homepage Copy Rules

- **Every section has one job.** If a section doesn't move the reader toward the CTA, cut it.
- **"You" language throughout.** The reader is the subject, not you.
- **One CTA, repeated.** Don't offer 5 different actions. Pick one and repeat it.
- **Social proof is specific.** "Went from 3 calls a month to 47" beats "great results."
- **No "welcome to our website."** Nobody reads it. Start with the value.

## Landing Page Conversion Rules

### The Landing Page Structure (single offer, single CTA)

1. **Headline** — the offer + the outcome, in one line. "Stop Wasting Google Ads Spend — Get a Full Funnel Audit."
2. **Subheadline** — the specific promise. "Find the leak in your ads, landing page, and tracking. Same-day, $49."
3. **The offer** — what they get, in a scannable list (bullets, not paragraphs).
4. **Proof** — one specific result or testimonial. "I fixed this for a moving company that went from $1M to $5M."
5. **Objection handling** — answer the top fear. "No changes to your account without approval."
6. **CTA** — one button, repeated. "Get the Audit."
7. **Risk reversal** — guarantee, refund, or "no contract" line.

### The Landing Page Rules

- **One offer, one audience, one CTA.** No navigation, no links to other pages, no "learn more about us."
- **Headline = the offer + the outcome.** Not the company name, not a welcome message.
- **Bullets over paragraphs.** A landing page is scanned, not read. Every benefit is a bullet.
- **Proof is specific and near the top.** Don't bury the case study at the bottom.
- **Objection handling is explicit.** Name the fear and answer it directly. "I've been burned before" → "We don't need a contract to keep you."
- **The CTA is a command, not a request.** "Get the Audit" beats "Submit."

### The Landing Page Headline Formulas

1. **Pain + Promise:** "Stop Wasting Ad Spend — Turn Clicks Into Calls"
2. **Offer + Outcome:** "Get a Full Funnel Audit — Find the Leak in 24 Hours"
3. **The "You" Confrontation:** "You're Paying for Clicks. You're Not Getting Calls."
4. **Specific Number:** "$49 Audit That Finds Where Your Ad Spend Leaks"

## The Conversion Copy Checklist (both pages)

Before shipping any homepage or landing page, verify:

- [ ] **One clear CTA** — is there a single action the page drives toward?
- [ ] **"You" language** — does the reader see themselves, or the company?
- [ ] **Specific proof** — numbers, names, or results, not vague claims?
- [ ] **Objection handled** — is the top fear named and answered?
- [ ] **Scannable** — bullets, short paragraphs, clear headings?
- [ ] **No fluff** — would a stranger understand the value in 5 seconds?
- [ ] **Pain first** — does it lead with the reader's problem, not the company's credentials?

---

## The "Would This Convert?" Test

After writing, ask three questions:

1. **If a stranger landed here, would they know what to do in 5 seconds?** If not, the CTA is buried.
2. **Would they feel understood?** If the copy doesn't name their pain, they'll bounce.
3. **Would they trust it?** If there's no specific proof, they won't act.

If any answer is "no," rewrite that section.
