# Copy Hackers Framework — Ghost-Writer

Copy Hackers is the school of conversion copywriting: every sentence earns its place, every word is aimed at the reader, and the goal is to make the reader feel understood before you make a point. Apply this to every draft.

## The Core Rules

### 1. Lead with the reader's pain, in "you" language

The first line is about THEM, not you. Not "we're excited to announce" — "you're losing money and you can't tell why."

**Bad:** "In this post, we'll explore the importance of conversion rate optimization."
**Good:** "You're getting traffic. You're not getting calls. And you can't figure out why."

**The test:** If you removed the first sentence, would the reader still know it's about them? If yes, rewrite it.

### 2. Make every sentence earn its place

No filler. No throat-clearing. No "in today's fast-paced world." If a sentence doesn't advance the pain, the proof, or the promise, cut it.

**The test:** Read each sentence. Does it make the reader feel something, learn something, or want to keep reading? If not, delete it.

### 3. "You" outnumbers "we" and "I"

Count the pronouns. The reader should see "you" and "your" far more than "we," "I," or "our." The copy is about their problem, not your credentials.

**Rule of thumb:** 3:1 ratio of "you" to "I/we" minimum.

### 4. Specific beats abstract

"$50,000 a year" beats "significant revenue loss." "3 calls a month" beats "low lead volume." "Tuesday at 2pm" beats "recently."

**The test:** Could a competitor swap their name into this sentence and it still be true? If yes, it's too abstract.

### 5. End on a specific next step

Every piece ends with a clear, concrete action — not "contact us for more information," but "here's the exact thing to do next."

**Bad:** "Reach out to learn more."
**Good:** "Open your Google Ads account, look at your search terms report, and find the first keyword that's costing you money without a conversion. That's your first leak."

## The Copy Hackers Structure (for pain-point blogs)

1. **The pain** — name the reader's problem in their own words
2. **The agitate** — make it hurt, show the cost of ignoring it
3. **The "you're not alone"** — normalize it, they're not the only one
4. **The reframe** — the real reason it's happening (not what they think)
5. **The proof** — a specific story, number, or example
6. **The promise** — what changes when they fix it
7. **The next step** — the concrete action

---

## Copy Hackers Anti-Patterns (Never Use)

- "We're excited to announce..." (nobody cares about your excitement)
- "In today's fast-paced world..." (throat-clearing)
- "As a leading provider of..." (self-congratulation)
- "Our team of experts..." (unproven claim)
- "Contact us today!" (weak, generic CTA)
- "We pride ourselves on..." (the reader doesn't care what you pride yourself on)
- "For more information, please..." (passive, dead)
- Any sentence that could be copy-pasted into a competitor's blog unchanged

---

## The One-Line Test

After writing, ask: **"If the reader only read the first line and the last line, would they know what's in it for them?"**

If the first line is about you and the last line is a weak CTA, the piece fails. The first line should hook their pain, the last line should tell them exactly what to do.
